# PRODUCT.md — DLX HAULER

## Product Purpose

A mobile-first PWA action-arcade game where you pilot a flying garbage hauler through a 2052 wasteland delivering cargo for the Dumplord X-Press shipping empire. Single-file canvas2D, no framework. The first product in a planned DLX brand portfolio (sister apps: FREIGHT, PAPER, MAIL, TAXI, etc. — Candy-Crush-style family).

## Users

Casual mobile gamers playing in 5–15 minute bursts on phones in dark/dim environments (couch, bed, transit). Genre fans of:
- Retro arcade (Paperboy, Crazy Taxi, Spy Hunter)
- Wasteland aesthetic (Mad Max, Borderlands, Death Stranding)
- Trucker culture / CB-radio nostalgia
- Indie auteur games with strong brand voice

Not for: AAA cinematic gamers, hyper-casual match-3 audience, kids.

## Register

**Brand.** The home page IS the marketing surface. It sells the world before it sells the game. Visual identity carries 100% of the first-touch impression — this is not a chrome shell around game content.

## Brand & Tone

- **Voice:** trucker dispatcher CB-radio. "10-4 good buddy." "Punch it." "Wheels fall off." "Roll out." Irreverent, blue-collar, lived-in. Never corporate. Never apologetic.
- **World:** 2052. Society's a smoking crater. The Dumplord still pushes crap and people still want it. You're DLX cargo crew. The badge is your uniform.
- **Aesthetic family:** retro-futuristic post-industrial. Construction-tape stripes, hazard chevrons, segmented displays, scuffed metal, neon signage. The opposite of clean SaaS or AAA cinematic.

## Strategic Principles

1. **Brand-forward over template.** Every screen looks like it could only belong to this game. If a generic "level select" template would fit, the design failed.
2. **Action-arcade chaos over polish.** Energy beats refinement. The home page should feel like a busted CB radio crackling with dispatch chatter, not a clean app shell.
3. **The world bleeds into the UI.** Buttons look like trucker dashboard switches. Stat panels look like cargo manifests. Loading bars look like fuel gauges. The diegesis is the design.
4. **Brand portfolio thinking.** Decisions made for HAULER's home page set patterns for sister apps (FREIGHT, etc.). Reusable but distinct: shared DLX badge + voice, different visual identity per game.

## Anti-references

- ❌ Generic mobile game home pages (Royal Match, Subway Surfers — clean cards, big play button center)
- ❌ App Store list-of-levels templates
- ❌ AAA game cinematic menus (Call of Duty, Destiny — too prestige, too "important")
- ❌ SaaS-clean dashboards with hero-metric tiles
- ❌ Generic flat-color minimalism
- ❌ Mid-2010s "skeuomorphism revival" (overlit chrome, drop shadows)
